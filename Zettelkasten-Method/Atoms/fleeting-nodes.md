In the Zettelkasten method, **Fleeting Notes** are the first and most basic level of note-taking. Think of them as the "capture" phase of your ideas.

Here is a breakdown of what they are, why they matter, and how to handle them.

---

### 1. Definition
Fleeting notes are temporary, quick reminders of thoughts or ideas. They are meant to capture a "spark" before it leaves your mind. They are not intended to be polished, well-organized, or even particularly clear to anyone but yourself in that specific moment.

### 2. Characteristics
*   **Quick:** You should be able to write them down in seconds.
*   **Unstructured:** They can be sentence fragments, bullet points, or even single words.
*   **Messy:** Grammar, spelling, and formatting do not matter.
*   **Temporary:** They have a very short shelf life (usually 24–48 hours).

### 3. The Purpose
The goal of a fleeting note is to **prevent the loss of an idea** while you are busy doing something else (like walking, driving, or in the middle of a meeting). Because our working memory is limited, these notes act as a "buffer" so you can stay focused on your current task without worrying about forgetting your new insight.

### 4. Where to Record Them
Fleeting notes can be captured anywhere that is convenient. Common tools include:
*   A small pocket notebook.
*   A voice memo on your phone.
*   A "Quick Capture" app (like Drafts, Apple Notes, or Todoist).
*   A napkin or a scrap of paper.

### 5. The Golden Rule: The Expiration Date
The most important rule of fleeting notes is that **they must be processed.** 

If you leave a fleeting note for a week, you will likely forget the context of why you wrote it. Within a day or two, you should look at your fleeting notes and ask:
1.  **Is this idea worth keeping?** If no, delete/discard it.
2.  **Does this relate to something I’m working on?** 
3.  **How can I turn this into a Permanent Note?**

### 6. The Transition
Once you have taken a fleeting note and decided it is valuable, you rewrite the idea in your own words as a **Literature Note** (if it came from a source) or a **Permanent Note** (if it is an original thought). 

**Once the idea is moved into your Zettelkasten, you delete or throw away the fleeting note.** They are the "scaffolding" used to build the building; once the building is standing, the scaffolding is removed.

### Summary Comparison
| Feature | Fleeting Note | Permanent Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Goal** | Capture a thought quickly | Store knowledge long-term |
| **Audience** | You (right now) | You (in 10 years) |
| **Quality** | Messy/Fragmented | Clear/Full sentences |
| **Lifespan** | 24–48 hours | Lifetime |
| **Location** | Anywhere (Inbox/Scraps) | Inside the Zettelkasten |
